No teal, no orange. The rest stays graphite, like Linear: colour only on the one piece that does the mending.

"Why this logo?"

It's a keystone, the last stone set in an arch. Until it goes in, the arch needs a wooden frame to hold it up. Once it's in, the stones lock against each other, the frame comes away, and the arch stands on its own. That's Bootmend's job: put the last piece right, then get out of the way.

  • The stone is caught just before it settles. The mark shows the moment of mending, not a finished monument. That's the one part of the shape that's a little off, on purpose.
  • No shield, broom, rocket or speedometer. Those are what "PC optimizers" use, and they promise protection or speed. An arch promises something quieter: built properly, it holds.
  • Colour on one piece only. Everything is graphite except the stone doing the work, the same way the app uses one colour for the one thing to press.
  • Built to be read small. It reads as an arch in one colour, blurred, and at 16 px in the taskbar, because the shape carries it, not the shading.
  • An arch is also a way in. Booting is where every PC starts. That's a bonus reading, not one the mark depends on.
